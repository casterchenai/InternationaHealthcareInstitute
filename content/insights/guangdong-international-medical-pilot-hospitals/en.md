## What was announced

The Guangdong Provincial Health Commission, Medical Insurance Bureau and Medical Products Administration jointly named **25 hospitals** as the province's first international medical service pilot institutions for **2026–2027**. The list includes Guangdong Provincial People's Hospital and the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University alongside other tertiary institutions across the GBA cities.

The pilot's stated aims are to raise the standard of service for Hong Kong, Macao, Taiwan and overseas patients, and — the operative part — to establish **direct settlement with Hong Kong insurers**, replacing the "pay first, claim later" arrangement that has been the main friction for Hong Kong residents seeking care on the mainland.

This follows Qianhai's report that its international medical services zone handled nearly **60,000 cross-border patient visits** in 2025 across 14 institutions and 4,045 beds, and completed the first instant insurance settlement for a Hong Kong patient. In January 2026 a secure data channel began feeding authorised records from designated Shenzhen hospitals into Hong Kong's eHealth system.

## Why it matters

For anyone building a business case on cross-border patients, three things changed:

- **Payment.** Direct settlement removes the largest single deterrent to using mainland services under a Hong Kong policy. Expect insurers to steer members to pilot hospitals.
- **Definition.** The pilot criteria — bilingual service, international-standard processes, insurer integration, data connectivity — are becoming the de facto definition of an "international" hospital in Guangdong. New entrants will be measured against them.
- **Data.** Records flowing into eHealth make follow-up in Hong Kong practical, which matters for procedures done on the mainland with recovery at home.

## What to do

- **Operators of existing Hong Kong-invested facilities** should assess whether they meet the pilot criteria and position for the next batch; designation is likely to become a marketing necessity.
- **New projects** should design insurer integration, bilingual pathways and data connectivity in from the start, rather than retrofitting them.
- **Hong Kong clinics** referring patients across the boundary should map which pilot hospitals cover their specialties and which insurers have signed settlement agreements.
