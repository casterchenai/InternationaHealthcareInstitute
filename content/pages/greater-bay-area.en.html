FAQ

Cross-border questions we hear most

Can a mainland or overseas company own a clinic in Hong Kong?

Yes. Under the Private Healthcare Facilities Ordinance (Cap. 633) the licensee of a clinic or day procedure centre may be a company, and there is no nationality restriction on ownership. What the regulator scrutinises is the facility: it must appoint a chief medical executive who is a medical practitioner registered in Hong Kong for at least four years, meet the Code of Practice on premises, staffing and equipment, and pass a Department of Health inspection. See the Hong Kong market-entry guide.

Can a Hong Kong company set up a wholly-owned hospital or clinic on the mainland?

Yes, through two routes. Hong Kong and Macao service providers have been able to establish wholly-owned hospitals under CEPA since 2013, and wholly-owned outpatient departments in Guangdong under provincial implementing rules. Since November 2024 a national pilot has also allowed wholly foreign-owned tertiary hospitals in nine regions including Guangzhou, Shenzhen and Hainan. Each route carries its own approval, staffing and scope conditions — the mainland guide compares them.

Which mainland zones offer the most support for Hong Kong medical operators?

Qianhai in Shenzhen runs a dedicated support scheme for Hong Kong and Macao medical institutions, revised in January 2026 and extended to foreign and Taiwan investors; Hengqin has special market-access measures aimed at Macao providers; Nansha in Guangzhou is a further pilot zone. Location should follow the clinical model and patient base first — subsidies are a secondary factor.

Does IHI handle mainland approvals directly?

IHI leads the feasibility, licence-pathway design, facility planning, standards, commissioning and staff training for cross-border projects, and coordinates a mainland professional network for local filings and approvals. In Hong Kong we act as your authorised representative with the Department of Health throughout the licence application.

How do the Elderly Health Care Voucher and 港澳藥械通 schemes affect a business case?

Both change demand. Voucher acceptance brings Hong Kong patients aged 65 and over to designated mainland institutions; 港澳藥械通 designation lets a mainland hospital use Hong Kong-registered drugs and devices that are not yet approved on the mainland. Designation is granted by Guangdong authorities in batches to qualifying institutions, so it is a planning objective rather than an automatic right — we build the eligibility criteria into the facility and governance design from the start.
