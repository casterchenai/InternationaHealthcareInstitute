Elderly Health Care Vouchers now accepted across all nine GBA cities: what it does to cross-border demand

The voucher scheme started as a Hong Kong subsidy for Hong Kong clinics. It is now one of the main channels sending Hong Kong patients to designated mainland institutions — and a template for how other schemes will be rolled out.

What changed

On 18 August 2025 the Health Bureau announced that the Elderly Health Care Voucher Greater Bay Area Pilot Scheme had been extended to Jiangmen, Huizhou and Zhaoqing, completing coverage of all nine mainland GBA cities. The scheme now has 19 institutions and 21 service points, all tertiary grade-A hospitals or equivalent, offering general outpatient and dental services to Hong Kong residents aged 65 and over.

The 2026-27 Budget (25 February 2026) then extended the voucher reward pilot — which credits extra value when vouchers are spent on preventive care — to the end of 2028. Separately, the Hospital Authority’s GBA patient pilot, which lets eligible public patients receive subsidised consultations at a designated collaborating institution, was extended to 31 March 2027.

Why it matters

Vouchers are demand with a Hong Kong Government guarantee behind it. Where a mainland institution can accept them, Hong Kong seniors — the highest-utilisation cohort — have a reason to cross the boundary that does not depend on price alone. The list of accepting institutions overlaps closely with the 港澳藥械通 designated list and, now, with Guangdong’s international medical service pilot hospitals, which is how a “cross-border-ready” tier of mainland institutions is forming.

For Hong Kong clinics the scheme is the other side of the same coin: the patients most likely to use vouchers on the mainland are those for whom a Hong Kong visit is inconvenient or expensive. Facilities near the boundary and in the north of the New Territories are exposed on both sides.

What to do

  • Mainland operators: participation is by designation, with criteria on grade, governance and Hong Kong-resident services. Build the file before applying.
  • Hong Kong operators: review how much of your elderly caseload is voucher-funded and price-sensitive, and whether a mainland partner or referral arrangement protects rather than cannibalises it.
  • Investors on either side: treat the voucher, HA pilot and insurer-settlement lists as the map of where cross-border demand is being institutionalised. Our policy tracker keeps them current.
