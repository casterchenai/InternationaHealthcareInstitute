Hong Kong's drug regulator takes shape: primary evaluation begins, CMPR due by end-2026

Hong Kong has relied on other regulators' approvals for decades. That is changing on a published timetable — and it affects clinics, day procedure centres and hospitals as much as pharmaceutical companies.

Where things stand

Hong Kong’s route to becoming a “primary” drug regulator — one that can assess a new medicine on its own data rather than waiting for approvals elsewhere — now has dates attached:

Step Status
“1+” mechanism: one reference-authority approval + local data In force since Nov 2023; extended to all new drugs Nov 2024; 20+ approvals to date
Primary evaluation Phase 1: extended applications of registered chemical entities In effect from 31 March 2026
Priority review for HA-recommended innovative drugs (100 working days) Announced June 2026
Hong Kong Centre for Medical Products Regulation (CMPR) as statutory regulator Legislation before LegCo; establishment targeted by end-2026
Statutory registration of medical devices Legislative proposal expected 2026
Full primary evaluation across product classes Roadmap to 2030

Why it matters to facilities

  • Formulary breadth. Faster registration means more products available to private facilities, particularly oncology and advanced therapies. Clinics offering infusion or specialist services will find their supply options widening.
  • Device regulation is coming. Medical devices are currently under a voluntary listing system. Statutory registration will change procurement, record-keeping and the evidence you need to hold for each device — a direct Code of Practice compliance issue for DPCs.
  • Cross-border. Products registered in Hong Kong are the feedstock for 港澳藥械通 on the mainland. A larger Hong Kong register means a larger catalogue that GBA designated hospitals can draw on.

What to do

  • Review your device inventory and documentation now, so that statutory registration does not catch an inspection-ready facility off guard.
  • If you run a specialist facility, track the “1+” approvals list and the HA innovative-drug pathway for products relevant to your specialty.
  • If you operate on both sides of the boundary, treat Hong Kong registration as part of your mainland product strategy.
