港澳藥械通 reaches 71 hospitals and a 115-product catalogue: what designation now requires

Five years after the first five hospitals were designated, 港澳藥械通 covers 71 institutions across all nine mainland GBA cities. The rules for joining are clearer — and stricter — than they were.

What changed

Designated institutions. On 8 December 2025 Guangdong published the fourth batch: 27 hospitals added and one removed, taking the total to 71 across all nine GBA mainland cities. Shenzhen gained seven, including Shenzhen People’s Hospital, Shenzhen Children’s Hospital and Qianhai Taikang Hospital. The province also re-verified the earlier batches — 44 passed, one was removed after ceasing operation — which signals that designation is now reviewed, not permanent.

Products. By 10 December 2025 Hong Kong’s Department of Health recorded 63 drugs and 77 devices approved for use under the scheme. On 2 February 2026 Guangdong issued the 2025 catalogue: 115 products (45 drugs, 70 devices), introducing for the first time a tier of 16 “leading” products — orphan drugs for conditions such as short-bowel syndrome, plus high-end equipment such as surgical robots and functional luminal imaging systems.

Speed and legal basis. Guangdong’s regulation on importing Hong Kong and Macao drugs and devices, in force since 1 December 2024, cut approval to 10 working days for catalogue items and 20 for urgent non-catalogue items.

Why it matters

For a Hong Kong-invested hospital or day procedure centre on the mainland, 港澳藥械通 designation is the difference between offering Hong Kong-standard treatment and offering a mainland formulary with a Hong Kong brand. It also feeds the cross-border patient schemes: voucher institutions, HA pilot partners and the new international medical service pilot hospitals overlap heavily with the designated list.

What designation requires in practice

Reading across the four batches and the re-verification, the recurring criteria are:

  • an operating institution (designation is applied for after the practice licence, not before);
  • pharmacy and device-management systems able to receive, store, trace and report imported products;
  • clinical governance for off-catalogue use — an ethics/clinical committee and adverse-event reporting;
  • a track record with cross-border patients or specialties with demonstrable unmet need.
