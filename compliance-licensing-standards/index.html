Private Healthcare Facilities Ordinance (Cap 633): Compliance Guide

The Private Healthcare Facilities Ordinance (Cap. 633) is a key piece of legislation that governs the operation of private healthcare facilities in Hong Kong. Its primary purpose is to ensure that healthcare services provided by private medical establishments, such as clinics, day procedure centres, and other healthcare providers, adhere to strict standards of safety, quality, and regulatory compliance. This ordinance is essential for maintaining public health and protecting patients by ensuring that private healthcare providers operate in a manner that meets established legal and ethical standards.

The Ordinance sets out the legal framework that regulates the establishment and operation of private healthcare facilities. It specifies the requirements for licensing, operations, and management of healthcare services, and includes detailed provisions related to:

  • Facility Licensing: The Ordinance requires all private healthcare facilities to obtain a valid licence from the relevant regulatory body before providing healthcare services. This includes ensuring that facilities meet prescribed safety standards, operational guidelines, and infrastructure requirements.
  • Operational Standards: The Ordinance's Code of Practice outlines the required operational standards for healthcare facilities, covering areas such as governance and management, staff credentials, patient care protocols, record keeping, facility design, and infection control measures. These standards are designed to ensure that healthcare services are delivered effectively, ethically, and in compliance with patient safety protocols.
  • Inspections and Compliance: The Ordinance empowers the authorities to conduct regular inspections of private healthcare facilities to ensure compliance with the regulations. Facilities found to be non-compliant with any aspect of the Ordinance may face penalties, suspension of licences, or even closure.
  • Patient Protection: One of the core goals of the Ordinance is to protect the well-being of patients. It includes provisions for safeguarding patient rights, ensuring adequate patient care, and implementing appropriate procedures in case of medical emergencies or incidents.

For healthcare providers operating in Hong Kong, understanding and complying with the Private Healthcare Facilities Ordinance is crucial. Non-compliance can result in significant legal and financial consequences, including loss of licence and reputational damage. As such, it is imperative for healthcare facilities to stay informed of updates to the Ordinance and its provisions.

Compliance Requirements for Clinics & Day Procedure Centres

Compliance with healthcare regulations is essential for maintaining safe, efficient, and legally-operating medical facilities. Clinics and day procedure centres in Hong Kong must adhere to specific governance standards, quality management protocols, and operational guidelines as defined by the Private Healthcare Facilities Ordinance and other relevant regulations. IHI offers specialised support to ensure that healthcare providers meet these legal and regulatory standards, ensuring their operations are both compliant and patient-centred. Below are the key compliance areas for clinics and day procedure centres.

Governance Standards

Effective governance is the foundation of any healthcare facility's operations. In Hong Kong, clinics and day procedure centres must adhere to stringent governance requirements to ensure transparent and accountable management. These governance standards cover a wide range of areas including:

  • Organisational Structure: Establishing clear roles and responsibilities within the facility, including the identification of the Licensee, Chief Medical Executive, Deputy Chief Medical Executives, and key personnel such as clinical specialty advisors, medical directors, facility managers, and compliance officers.
  • Internal Policies: Developing and implementing internal policies that guide day-to-day operations, staff responsibilities, patient care protocols, and emergency procedures. These policies must align with both local regulations and industry best practices.
  • Leadership Accountability: Ensuring that the leadership team is responsible for the continuous monitoring of operational performance, compliance audits, and regular reviews to meet regulatory standards.
  • Reporting and Documentation: Maintaining accurate and comprehensive records that document patient care, facility maintenance, and compliance with health regulations. The governance framework should also include periodic audits to assess adherence to standards.

IHI assists healthcare providers in implementing robust governance frameworks that meet regulatory requirements, ensuring that your clinic or day procedure centre remains compliant and operates smoothly.

Quality and Safety Management

In healthcare settings, the quality of care and patient safety are paramount. Clinics and day procedure centres must implement comprehensive quality and safety management systems to ensure that patients receive the highest standards of care while minimising risk. Key components include:

  • Clinical Care Standards: Clear guidelines for medical procedures, patient assessments, treatment protocols, and clinical outcomes, regularly reviewed and updated in line with evolving healthcare practices.
  • Patient Safety Protocols: Policies that prioritise patient safety at every stage of care, from initial consultation to post-procedure recovery — including infection control procedures, the use of personal protective equipment (PPE), and measures to prevent medical errors.
  • Incident Reporting and Risk Management: A system for reporting and analysing incidents, adverse events, and near-misses, with corrective actions taken to improve safety and mitigate future risks.
  • Staff Training and Competency: Ensuring that all staff members, from medical professionals to support staff, are properly trained and have the necessary skills to adhere to safety standards. Regular training and evaluations are key to maintaining a high level of care.
  • Continuous Improvement: A system for continuous quality improvement (CQI), including regular audits, patient feedback mechanisms, and the use of performance metrics to identify areas for improvement.

IHI's expertise in quality and safety management helps clinics and day procedure centres design and implement effective systems that promote patient safety, improve clinical outcomes, and ensure compliance with healthcare regulations.
