FAQ

Clinic licensing in Hong Kong, answered

Does my clinic need a licence under Cap. 633?

Under the Private Healthcare Facilities Ordinance (Cap. 633), private premises in Hong Kong that provide medical services generally require a clinic licence — or a day procedure centre licence where scheduled medical procedures are performed. Some small practices run by an individual practitioner may instead qualify for a letter of exemption. Confirming which category applies to you is the first thing we do in the free assessment.

How long does a clinic licence application take?

For a new facility the application should be submitted at least two months before you plan to operate. Once the Department of Health confirms the documents are complete it typically schedules an inspection within about ten days, and issues its decision within fifteen working days of the inspection. The overall timeline depends mostly on how ready your premises, staffing and documentation are — which is exactly what the gap audit establishes.

What if my premises don't meet the requirements yet?

That is common. Our gap audit lists exactly what falls short of the Code of Practice — layout, ventilation, equipment, records or staffing — and sets out a prioritised rectification plan. Where physical works are needed we work with experienced healthcare engineering contractors so the works are done once, to the standard the inspector expects.

Do you help mainland or overseas investors set up in Hong Kong?

Yes. We advise investors from the Greater Bay Area and overseas on the licence category, premises requirements, staffing and the appointment of a Chief Medical Executive, and we act as your authorised representative with the Department of Health throughout. Our team communicates in English and Chinese.

What does the free initial assessment include?

You tell us about your planned or existing facility on WhatsApp. We confirm the licence category that applies, outline the key requirements and likely obstacles, and propose a pathway with an indicative timeline. There is no charge and no obligation.

What is the difference between a clinic licence and a day procedure centre licence?

Broadly, a clinic licence covers premises providing consultations and minor treatments, while a day procedure centre licence is required where scheduled medical procedures listed by the regulator — such as endoscopy or certain surgical and aesthetic procedures — are performed without overnight stay. DPC requirements are substantially stricter. If you are unsure which applies, that is the first thing we resolve in your free assessment.
